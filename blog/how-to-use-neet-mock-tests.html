How to use NEET mock tests to improve your score

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Take one full-length mock test a week in the same 3-hour slot as NEET, then spend as much time on analysis as on the test. Write every mistake into a list, revise those topics, and re-check them in the next test. In the last two months, move to two or three tests a week.

1. When to start

Start chapter-wise tests as soon as you finish a chapter, and full-length tests once most of the syllabus is done once. Waiting until "everything is perfect" leaves too little time to fix weak areas.

2. Take the test like the real exam

  • Same time of day as NEET, 3 hours, no breaks, phone notifications off.
  • Follow your section order and time plan every time.

3. Analyse every test

  • Sort mistakes into three groups: did not know, silly error, ran out of time.
  • Read the solution for every wrong and guessed question.
  • Look at the chapter-wise analysis and pick the two weakest chapters for the week.

4. Track progress, not one score

Scores go up and down with paper difficulty. Your rank among other students and your accuracy over five or six tests show the real trend.

Frequently asked questions

How many mock tests should I take before NEET?

One full test a week for most of the year and two or three a week in the last two months is a common plan; the analysis matters more than the number.

Are online mock tests useful if NEET is on paper?

Yes. They train speed, time management and accuracy under a timer; practise filling an OMR sheet separately before the exam.

What should I do after a mock test?

Read every solution, write your mistakes into a list, revise those topics, and check them again in the next test.
